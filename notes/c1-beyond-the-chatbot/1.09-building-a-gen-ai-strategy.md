# 1.9 Building a successful gen AI strategy
Course 1 · activity 638559 · contains a video (summarised)

## Core message (video)
- You don't have to be AI-first like Google, but gen AI should be **at the forefront of strategy**. Only you know your business needs.
- Don't adopt new technology headfirst without a plan. Also consider how the **structure of the business must change** to implement gen AI.
- **Recommended: a two-fold approach, top-down AND bottom-up at the same time.**
  - **Top-down:** set a clear, compelling AI vision aligned with **strategic business priorities**. Start with priorities and the most important use cases and workflows. *Only then* ask how AI supports them.
  - **Bottom-up:** encourage teams to share challenges and gen AI ideas. The best ideas often come from those **closest to the problem**. Encourage small-scale use, then share solutions for broader rollout.
  - **Why both:**
    - Top-down alone misses opportunities.
    - Top-down gives the bottom-up experiments (1) a place in the overall strategy and (2) a **safe and responsible** frame.

## Roles
- **Executives and high-level management:**
  - **executive sponsorship** and a **clear vision**;
  - align initiatives with business goals;
  - secure **adequate resources** and organisation-wide support;
  - empower employees to experiment.
- **Mid-level managers and individual contributors (ICs):**
  - close to daily operations and end users, which gives them insight;
  - champion adoption by finding **high-impact, feasible** solutions to specific workflow challenges;
  - encourage experimentation and feedback so tools fit existing processes.

## Multi-directional strategy: six areas, both levels
| Area | Executives / leadership | Mid-level managers & ICs |
|---|---|---|
| **Strategic focus** | Focus on one high-impact area first, succeed, then expand (maximises learning and scalability). Prioritise use cases that are **feasible, actionable, affordable, with high anticipated business value / ROI**. | Start from the most pressing **pain points** (your own or customers'). Understand user needs, workflows and expectations: **human-centred design**. |
| **Exploration** | Explore **low-risk** areas first. Empower experimentation (e.g. **hackathons**, dedicated time and resources). Foster sharing of findings and best practices. | Experiment with different tools to learn capabilities and limits. Share results. If successful, widen scope and bring insights to leadership. |
| **Responsible AI** | Set **ethical guidelines** and safety mechanisms so gen AI is used responsibly and securely.<br>Apply robust **data governance** (security, privacy, regulations) with enterprise tooling: **Google Cloud Data Loss Prevention** to discover, classify and de-identify data, **Cloud Key Management Service** to manage encryption keys, **Google SecOps** to detect and respond to threats.<br>Adopt a **content moderation policy** in which employees review AI-generated content, especially in sensitive or high-risk uses.<br>Monitor performance and impact regularly to catch unintended consequences or ethical concerns. | Follow company AI standards and organisational values. Anticipate pitfalls proactively. Test and evaluate thoroughly for bias, safety and ethics issues. |
| **Resourcing** | Build a robust **data strategy** so teams can access high-quality, relevant data for training and fine-tuning gen AI models.<br>Don't reinvent the wheel: reuse existing tools and platforms where possible.<br>Invest in **AI talent**: upskill existing employees and hire people with gen AI expertise. | Use resources already in the team (datasets, tools, platforms). If more is needed, make the case to leadership on value and ROI. |
| **Impact** | Set clear goals and **KPIs** (e.g. increased sales, fewer complaints). Communicate progress and impact to stakeholders to justify investment. | Demonstrate and **quantify** tangible impact (efficiency, satisfaction, cost savings). Link work to org goals. Track KPIs to refine the approach. |
| **Continuous improvement** | Develop **iteratively**, refining solutions based on feedback, performance data and changing business needs.<br>Set a regular **evaluation cadence** to assess the quality, accuracy and effectiveness of outputs, and use it to guide improvements.<br>Set up mechanisms to gather feedback from users and stakeholders. | Test, measure and refine continuously, based on user feedback and real-world performance.<br>Update solutions regularly with new data and feedback.<br>Keep learning: take courses, read articles, experiment. |

## Knowledge check: who is primarily responsible?
- **Executives / high-level management:** setting overall vision and strategy; securing funding and resources; establishing ethical guidelines.
- **Mid-level managers / ICs:** identifying use cases in daily workflows; experimenting with tools.

## Key takeaway
Success needs a **combined top-down and bottom-up strategy**. Leaders set vision and provide resources. Teams find practical applications and experiment. Collaboration and continuous improvement sustain it.

## Exam hooks
- Classic scenario: "Who should …?" → apply the split above.
- Prioritisation criteria: feasible, actionable, affordable, high value / ROI. Start **low-risk**.
