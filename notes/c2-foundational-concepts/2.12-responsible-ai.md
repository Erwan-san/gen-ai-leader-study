# 2.12 Responsible AI
Course 2 · activity 607923 · no video

## Definition
- **Responsible AI** = ensuring AI apps **avoid intentional AND unintentional harm**. It covers ethical development and positive outcomes.

## Security is the foundation
- **Responsible AI is built on security.** Secure apps protect both the company and its users.
- Analogy: a house with a weak foundation is compromised however beautiful the design.

## Pillars covered
1. **Transparency:** users must understand **how their information is used** and **how the AI works**: data handling, decision processes, potential biases.
2. **Privacy:**
   - **anonymise or pseudonymise** data so individuals can't easily be identified;
   - models can **leak sensitive training data**, so add safeguards.
3. **Data quality, bias and fairness:**
   - Responsible AI needs **high-quality data**. Inaccurate or incomplete data produces biased outcomes.
   - Technology reflects **societal biases**, and AI can **amplify** them.
   - Also ask whether the data was **collected consensually** and whether it could **perpetuate harmful bias**.
   - Example: a **resume-screening tool** favouring one demographic because of historical hiring data. (Analogy: a dog trained with biased commands.)
   - **Fairness must be a core principle.**
4. **Accountability and explainability:**
   - **Fairness requires accountability:** know **who is responsible** for the AI's actions and **how it decides**.
   - **Explainable AI** makes model decisions **transparent and understandable**. That builds trust, helps debugging and uncovers hidden bias. (Analogy: a judge explaining a verdict.)
   - Tool: **Google Cloud's Vertex Explainable AI**.
5. **Legal implications:**
   - Key areas: **data privacy, non-discrimination, intellectual property, product liability**.
   - Laws mandate responsible data handling, bias mitigation and transparency in algorithmic decisions.
   - Respect the **licences, rules and limitations of the models you use**.
   - The landscape is **evolving fast**: stay informed and seek **legal counsel**. Compliance is part of building **trustworthy** AI, not just a hurdle.

## Flashcard answers
- Why is data quality crucial? → To avoid **biased or discriminatory outcomes**.
- Purpose of explainable AI? → To make **AI decision-making transparent and understandable**.
- Key legal considerations? → **Data privacy, non-discrimination, intellectual property**.
- How do AI systems perpetuate societal bias? → Through **biased training data, biased developers, or biased deployment environments**.

## Course 2 wrap-up
Consider **safety, security and ethics at every stage** of the lifecycle and actively mitigate issues. With the right guardrails, AI benefits society.

## Exam hooks
- **Secure AI = intentional harm** (attacks). **Responsible AI = intentional + unintentional harm.** Security is the base of responsible AI.
- Explainability tool → **Vertex Explainable AI**.

## Exam wording & corrections
- Exam guide wording: 'data **anonymization** and **pseudonymization**'. Anonymization removes identifiers so data can't be linked back to a person; pseudonymization swaps identifiers for tokens that can only be re-linked with separately protected information.
