## Key points
- Business data helps organisations **understand customers, optimise operations, drive strategy**.
- **Larger datasets often improve performance**, especially for complex gen AI. But performance also depends on the data's **type, volume, organisation and structure**.
- Knowing your company's data (**quality, availability, form**) sets the boundary of what's possible with AI.

## Structured vs unstructured data
| | Structured | Unstructured |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | Organised into a predefined format (rows and columns); easy to search | No predefined structure; messy and complex; needs more sophisticated analysis |
| Storage | Often a **relational database** (tables, a "digital filing cabinet") | Files, object storage |
| Examples | Phone contact list, online shopping orders, bank statements, CSV sales tables | **Text** (PDFs, emails, social posts), **images** (photos, digital art, medical scans), **audio** (speech, music), **video** (movies, YouTube, phone videos) |

**Example company: Cymbal Cleaning** (fictitious eco-friendly cleaning supplies). Order data includes customer ID, name, purchase date, order cost, delivery address, product image, and feedback on a 1–5 star scale.
- Structured: IDs, name, dates, cost, address, star rating.
- Unstructured: product image, **free-form feedback text**, **email content**.

## Knowledge check answers
| Item | Type |
|---|---|
| A product review sentence on an e-commerce site | Unstructured |
| CSV of Date / Product ID / Quantity / Price | Structured |
| Audio recording of a support call | Unstructured |
| Digital image of a cat | Unstructured |
| Medical record fields: date of birth, address, vaccination history | Structured |

## Key takeaway
Understanding your data is the **first step** of any AI implementation. The **type, quality and structure** of data determine what's possible.
