## Definitions
- **Foundation models:** large AI models trained on enormous datasets. That broad training gives them a deep understanding of the data, so they can be **adapted to a wide range of downstream tasks**. The course calls them a **paradigm shift** in AI: they streamline processes, automate tasks and unlock new possibilities across business functions.
- **Artificial intelligence (AI):** computer systems performing tasks that typically need human intelligence (understanding language, making decisions).
- **Machine learning (ML):** the usual way to achieve AI. Systems **learn from data** to solve specific problems.
- **Traditional AI models vs foundation models:**
  - Traditional models are trained for a **single purpose** on specific data (e.g. a spam filter).
  - Foundation models are trained on massive, **diverse** data (text, images, code), so they adapt to many tasks. This flexibility is what makes them groundbreaking.
- **LLMs (large language models)** are the best-known type of foundation model. They are a **specialised type of foundation model focused on language**.

## Key features of foundation models
1. **Trained on diverse data:** they learn general patterns and relationships that transfer across tasks.
2. **Flexible:** one model supports a wide range of use cases.
3. **Adaptable:** they can be specialised for domains or use cases through additional, targeted training.

## Example foundation models (and their training data)
| Model | Training data | Typical tasks |
|---|---|---|
| **Gemini** | Massive multimodal data: text, images, code, audio, video, more | Language understanding, image generation, code generation, cross-domain tasks |
| **Nano Banana** | Mainly images and text descriptions | Text-to-image generation, image editing, understanding image content |
| **Chirp** | Audio in many languages | Speech recognition: voice assistants, transcription, translation |

## Knowledge checks in the lesson (answer key in my words)
- **Foundation model or not?**
  - Foundation models: a deep-learning text-to-image generator; a model trained on vast image and video data that understands, generates and answers questions about visual content; a model trained on massive code data that can also translate and write creative content.
  - **Not** foundation models: a **decision tree** for churn prediction; a **linear regression** for house prices. These are narrow, single-task models.
- **Foundation model vs LLM:** the two correct statements are "All LLMs are foundation models" and "LLMs are a specialised type of foundation model". Not every foundation model is an LLM.

## Key takeaway
Foundation models are **large-scale, general-purpose** models trained on massive data and adaptable to many downstream tasks. Traditional ML models are task-specific and trained on smaller, focused datasets.
