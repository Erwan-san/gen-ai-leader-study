# 2.2 Understanding AI, ML, and generative AI
Course 2 · activity 607911 · video summarised

## Definitions (course wording, condensed)
- **Artificial intelligence (AI):** the broad field of building machines that perform tasks requiring human intelligence: learning, problem-solving, decision-making.
- **Machine learning (ML):** a **subset of AI**. Machines **learn from data** to perform specific tasks.
- **Generative AI:** described here both as an **application of AI** and, in the video, as a **subset of ML**. It **creates new content** (images, text, music).

## The model = the math
- A **model** is essentially the math: input in, output out, like an equation.
- The model is **built from the data**, so **different data creates different models**. That's why data matters so much.
- Real models are more complex: many **parameters** and algorithms, "more like a system of equations".

## Predictive (traditional) AI vs generative AI
- **Traditional AI** analyses data to **predict or classify** an outcome (e.g. spam or not spam).
- **Gen AI** learns the underlying **patterns and structures** of its training data and generates **new, similar content**.
- **Painting analogy:**
  - Traditional AI: the child is shown what a good painting looks like and learns to **judge** paintings.
  - Gen AI: the child sees many paintings, gets brushes, and **creates** a new one.
- Gen AI needs **very large amounts of data** to learn those patterns well.

## Recap hierarchy
**AI** (broad field) → **ML** (a method within AI) → **gen AI** (an application or subset that creates new content).
Non-technical leaders don't need the maths, but they do need these concepts.
